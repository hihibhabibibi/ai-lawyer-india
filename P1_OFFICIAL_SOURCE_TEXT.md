## 2. `general-02` — Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, section 12

**Official source (India Code PDF):** https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/16128/1/198739.pdf

**Exact relevant statutory text (section 12):**
> 12. Criteria for giving legal services.—Every person who has to file or defend a case shall be entitled to legal services under this Act if that person is—
> (a) a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe;
> (b) a victim of trafficking in human beings or begar as referred to in article 23 of the Constitution;
> (c) a woman or a child;
> (d) a person with disability ...
> (e) a person under circumstances of undeserved want such as being a victim of a mass disaster, ethnic violence, caste atrocity, flood, drought, earthquake or industrial disaster; or
> (f) an industrial workman; or
> (g) in custody; or
> (h) a person in receipt of annual income less than the amount prescribed by the relevant Government ...

**Additional official source for current UP income ceiling:** https://nalsa.gov.in/legal-aid/  
NALSA's current page lists Uttar Pradesh at ₹3,00,000 annual income for cases before courts other than the Supreme Court. The income ceiling is state-specific and may change.

## 4. `wages-01` — Code on Wages, 2019, section 17

**Official Act PDF:** https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/the_code_on_wages_2019_no._29_of_2019.pdf  
**Official commencement notification:** https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/e-noti-wage.pdf

**Exact relevant statutory text (section 17):**
> 17. Time limit for payment of wages.—(1) The employer shall pay or cause to be paid wages to the employees, engaged on—
> (i) daily basis, at the end of the shift;
> (ii) weekly basis, on the last working day of the week, that is to say, before the weekly holiday;
> (iii) fortnightly basis, before the end of the second day after the end of the fortnight;
> (iv) monthly basis, before the expiry of the seventh day of the succeeding month.
> (2) Where an employee has been—
> (i) removed or dismissed from service; or
> (ii) retrenched or has resigned from service, or became unemployed due to closure of the establishment,
> the wages payable to him shall be paid within two working days of his removal, dismissal, retrenchment or, as the case may be, his resignation.

**Commencement check:** Gazette notification S.O. 5322(E), dated 21 November 2025, brought sections 1–41 and 43–66 into force from 21 November 2025. Section 17 is within sections 1–41.

## 7. `consumer-01` — Consumer Protection Act, 2019, section 2 definitions

**Official Act PDF (India Code-hosted):** https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_UK_89_1171_00002_00002_1630393876767&filename=a2019-35.pdf&type=actfile

**Exact relevant text (section 2(7), consumer — excerpt):**
> “consumer” means any person who—
> (i) buys any goods for a consideration which has been paid or promised or partly paid and partly promised, or under any system of deferred payment and includes any user of such goods other than the person who buys such goods ... when such use is made with the approval of such person, but does not include a person who obtains such goods for resale or for any commercial purpose; or
> (ii) hires or avails of any service for a consideration which has been paid or promised or partly paid and partly promised, or under any system of deferred payment ... but does not include a person who avails of such service for any commercial purpose.
> Explanation.— ... “buys any goods” and “hires or avails any services” includes offline or online transactions through electronic means ...

**Exact relevant text (section 2(10), defect — excerpt):**
> “defect” means any fault, imperfection or shortcoming in the quality, quantity, potency, purity or standard which is required to be maintained by or under any law for the time being in force or under any contract, express or implied or as is claimed by the trader in any manner whatsoever in relation to any goods ...

**Exact relevant text (section 2(11), deficiency — excerpt):**
> “deficiency” means any fault, imperfection, shortcoming or inadequacy in the quality, nature and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law for the time being in force or has been undertaken to be performed by a person in pursuance of a contract or otherwise in relation to any service and includes—
> (i) any act of negligence or omission or commission by such person which causes loss or injury to the consumer; and
> (ii) deliberate withholding of relevant information by such person.

## 8. `consumer-03` — Consumer Commission pecuniary jurisdiction

**Official Gazette / Department of Consumer Affairs PDF:** https://consumeraffairs.nic.in/sites/default/files/jurisdicton%20of%20Consumer%20Commissions.pdf  
**Gazette copy:** https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2021/232278.pdf

**Exact relevant Rules 3–5 (2021 Rules):**
> 3. Jurisdiction of District Commission.— ... the District Commission shall have jurisdiction to entertain complaints where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration does not exceed fifty lakh rupees.
> 4. Jurisdiction of State Commission.— ... where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration exceeds fifty lakh but does not exceed two crore rupees.
> 5. Jurisdiction of National Commission.— ... where the value of the goods or services paid as consideration, exceeds two crore rupees.

**Important correction:** The original unamended text of section 34(1) says ₹1 crore, but the 2021 Rules made under the Act prescribe the current tiers above. Use the Rules, not the original section 34 figure, for the current pecuniary thresholds. Section 34(2) provides territorial jurisdiction options, including the complainant's residence.

## 11. `consumer-06` — Consumer Protection Act, 2019, section 39

**Official Government PDF:** https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0430192e936ba11d0a202097fed8f4/uploads/2023/11/2023110898.pdf

**Exact relevant statutory text (section 39(1), selected remedies):**
> 39. Findings of District Commission.—(1) Where the District Commission is satisfied that the goods complained against suffer from any of the defects specified in the complaint or that any of the allegations contained in the complaint about the services or any unfair trade practices, or claims for compensation under product liability are proved, it shall issue an order to the opposite party directing him to do one or more of the following, namely:—
> (a) to remove the defect ...
> (b) to replace the goods with new goods of similar description which shall be free from any defect;
> (c) to return to the complainant the price, or, as the case may be, the charges paid by the complainant along with such interest on such price or charges as may be decided;
> (d) to pay ... compensation to the consumer for any loss or injury suffered by the consumer due to the negligence of the opposite party ...

The remainder of section 39(1) lists further remedies, including removal of service deficiencies, discontinuance of unfair/restrictive trade practices, withdrawal of hazardous goods and costs. A refund is a remedy the Commission may order where the statutory conditions are met; it is not automatic in every dispute.

## 12. `deposit-01` — Security deposit: use the enacted Uttar Pradesh statute for UP cases

**Official Uttar Pradesh Gazette / India Code PDF:** https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/21157/1/english_16_of_2021.pdf  
**Central Model Tenancy Act (model text only):** https://mohua.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Model-Tenancy-Act-English-02_06_2021.pdf

**Exact relevant text (U.P. Regulation of Urban Premises Tenancy Act, 2021, section 11):**
> 11. (1) The security deposit to be paid by the tenant in advance shall be such as may be agreed upon between the landlord and the tenant in the tenancy agreement, which shall—
> (a) not exceed two months’ rent, in case of residential premises; and
> (b) not exceed six months’ rent, in case of non-residential premises.
> (2) The security deposit shall be refunded to the tenant on the date of taking over vacant possession of the premises from the tenant, after making due deduction of any liability of the tenant.

**Applicability warning:** This is a Uttar Pradesh state enactment, not a rule for all India. Section 1 defines its geographic application and section 3 lists excluded premises. The central Model Tenancy Act is a model shared with States/UTs for adoption; it is not by itself a directly applicable all-India tenancy statute.

## 13. `deposit-03` — Indian Contract Act, 1872, section 73

**Official India Code PDF:** https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2187/2/A187209.pdf

**Exact statutory text:**
> 73. Compensation for loss or damage caused by breach of contract.—When a contract has been broken, the party who suffers by such breach is entitled to receive, from the party who has broken the contract, compensation for any loss or damage caused to him thereby, which naturally arose in the usual course of things from such breach, or which the parties knew, when they made the contract, to be likely to result from the breach of it.
> Such compensation is not to be given for any remote and indirect loss or damage sustained by reason of the breach.

**KB warning:** Section 73 may support a contractual damages claim where the agreement is breached. It does not, by itself, establish an automatic right to the whole security deposit in every case. Read the agreement and the applicable tenancy statute.

## 14. `deposit-04` — Limitation Act, 1963, section 3 and Schedule

**Official India Code PDF:** https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1565/5/A1963-36.pdf

**Exact relevant statutory text (section 3):**
> 3. Bar of limitation.—(1) Subject to the provisions contained in sections 4 to 24 (inclusive), every suit instituted, appeal preferred, and application made after the prescribed period shall be dismissed, although limitation has not been set up as a defence.

**Important limitation warning:** Do not label every security-deposit claim as governed by one Schedule article without examining the tenancy agreement, the date vacant possession was handed over, the date repayment became due, any demand/refusal, and the legal basis pleaded. Article 55 (compensation for breach of contract) or the residuary Article 113 may be relevant depending on the cause of action; a lawyer should confirm the correct article and accrual date. The three-year period must not be described as starting automatically from the date the tenant paid the deposit.
